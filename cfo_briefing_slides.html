Slide 1  ·  Why the 96 % Cannot Be Trusted

The internally reported R² of 96 % is the result of four systematic methodological errors — not genuine predictive power.
Flaw 1 · Target Leakage (rolling average)
The 3-day and 7-day rolling revenue averages included today's actual revenue in the feature. The model learned to read the answer from the question — trivially inflating accuracy.
Flaw 2 · Temporal Leakage (random split)
Training and test data were randomly shuffled across all years. A model trained on 2023 data was "validated" on 2014 data — the opposite of real forecasting. Lag features of training rows directly encoded test labels.
Flaw 3 · Prospective Leakage (same-day KPIs)
Average basket value and items-per-order are end-of-day outcomes. They cannot exist at the moment a forecast is made, making the model operationally unusable as built.
Flaw 4 · Preprocessing Leakage (scaler fitted on full dataset)
StandardScaler.fit_transform(X) was called on the entire dataset before the train/test split. The scaler's mean and std are contaminated by test-set statistics, violating train/test independence. Fix: fit only on training data, then transform-only on test data.
Correcting all four flaws brings R² from 0.96 (inflated) → 0.91 (honest) on genuinely unseen future data.
